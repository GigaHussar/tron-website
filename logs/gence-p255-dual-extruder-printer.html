Getting a Cheap Dual-Extruder Industrial 3D Printer Running Right

April–August 2026

#3d_printing#hardware

I was browsing an online marketplace for printer deals, mostly out of habit. I know most of the models people try to sell secondhand, and usually none of them interest me. This time one listing stood out, especially at the price being asked, so I bought it for 800zł.

Once it arrived, it was clear why it felt like a deal: this thing is built like an industrial machine. Metal frame, dual all-metal hotends, ball screws on the Z axis instead of the usual threaded rods, and aluminum carriages riding on all axes. It even has a proper filament runout sensor — one that actually detects filament moving through it, not just a microswitch that only checks whether filament is present at all.

The software side was a different story. It ran some Marlin-style firmware with a bunch of custom commands layered on top, paired with its own dedicated slicer. I ditched that slicer almost immediately in favor of Orca Slicer. Porting the print settings over took a bit of time, but it was worth it for the extra control Orca gives me over the print process.

The seller mentioned they had only ever printed using one of the two nozzles, so I had no idea if the second one even worked. First thing I noticed when I looked closer was that the small metal part meant to physically plug the idle nozzle was bent out of shape. Easy fix — I just bent it back into the correct position.

Getting prints onto the machine turned into its own small saga. I first tried printing over USB using Repetier Host on my PC, with a 3 meter cable, and it failed. I tried again with a 1.5 meter cable and got noticeably better results — shorter USB cables suffer less signal degradation, which matters a lot when you're streaming gcode over a live serial connection instead of reading it off local storage. But cable length wasn't the real problem. The printer handled USB printing strangely: it ignored the tool offsets and Z offset saved on the printer itself, applying different values instead. I tried to correct it by editing the gcode directly, and the printer responded by halting the print entirely, apparently convinced it was about to crash the nozzle into the bed. I gave up on USB printing and bought an SD card to print from instead, only to find the mechanism that holds the card in its slot doesn't grip properly. I might end up buying a small device to connect there and send prints directly over network instead of fighting the SD slot.

Somewhere in the middle of all this the nozzle got blocked. The first layer was printing too close to the bed, most likely because the bed wasn't correctly leveled. I tried running the printer's auto bed leveling to fix it properly, but that routine kept throwing errors partway through. In the end I just increased the Z offset manually in the printer's settings, which cleared the problem without needing the auto calibration to cooperate.

Between the slicer swap, the bent nozzle plug, the USB troubles, and the offset fiddling, it took a while before this printer felt properly dialed in. But for 800zł and this build quality, I can't really complain about the learning curve.

Bypassing a Dead Charging Dock on My Roborock S5

July 2026

#hardware#smart_home

My Roborock S5 — the same one I rooted with Valetudo a while back — always had a mild charging problem. It would dock, then after some time started repositioning. Annoying, but livable. Then one day it charged normally for about five minutes and just stopped. A restart didn't help. It wouldn't charge at all after that, docked or not, and it kept telling me to clean the charging contacts.

I had already cleaned them, more than once, so I didn't believe that was the actual problem this time. I went looking for the specific failure and found a guide pointing at a known weak spot in these docks: a capacitor that degrades and eventually takes the charging circuit down with it. Made sense given the symptoms. I read through the repair steps, thought about it for a day or two, and decided against it. I don't have the soldering skills or the tools to do that reliably, and even a clean capacitor swap wouldn't guarantee a fix if something else on the board had gone with it.

I looked at buying a replacement dock instead. The price was almost exactly what I'd paid for the whole vacuum used. That killed the idea — spending that much on a dock for a robot I got cheap specifically because it was older felt backwards.

So I started thinking about skipping the dock's charging circuit entirely. From what I read, the dock just feeds the vacuum 20V DC through the two charging pads — no obvious reason to expect anything smarter than that, though I couldn't fully rule out some handshake between dock and robot before I tried. I had an old laptop charger rated at 19.5V DC, close enough, with plenty of amperage to spare. Given the model's age and the simplicity of the interface, the plan was straightforward: open the dock, cut the two wires running to the charging pads, and wire the laptop charger's positive and negative straight onto them.

It worked. No handshake, no smart pairing, just DC into contacts. The vacuum charged fine.

One side effect showed up afterward: it now has trouble returning to the dock and docking correctly, something it didn't do before. I'm not sure if that's actually caused by the bypass or just a coincidence with the same underlying fault, but it's the one thing nagging at me. Still, I'll take a vacuum that charges with an occasional docking fumble over one that doesn't charge at all.
